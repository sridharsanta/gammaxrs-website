01Foundation

A production company with a research department

Gamma XRS sits in an unusual place: we run the stage and we build the software that drives it. The identity has to carry both without splitting in two.

Positioning

Gamma XRS operates LED volumes and builds the real-time pipeline behind them — camera tracking, colour management, scene delivery. We are the only party on a production who owns both the stage and the code running on it, which is why the image holds up when the schedule doesn't.

Promise

Final pixel, on the day.

Six words. Use as the sign-off on decks, the hero line on the site, and the tagline in the stacked lockup. Do not paraphrase it — a promise that changes wording stops being a promise.

Principles

These govern the work before they govern the design. When a brand decision is contested, resolve it against this list in order.

P1
Photoreal is a measurement, not an opinion.We publish our colour maths, our latency figures and our tracking tolerances. Marketing claims must be traceable to a number someone on the floor can reproduce.
P2
The stage serves the story.Technology is never the subject of our communication. The frame it produced is. Lead with the shot; put the rig in the caption.
P3
Ship the tool, not the demo.We show software that crews use in production, in the state crews use it. No mocked interfaces, no aspirational screenshots.
P4
Colour is a contract.Our palette is defined in hex, in Rec.709 and in P3, and the spectrum ramp is a fixed sequence of five stops. It is not a mood — it is a spec other people build against.

The identity runs in two expressions. They share the marks, the palette and the typefaces exactly — what changes is density, how much of the spectrum is on screen, and whether the page leads with an image or a diagram.

Practitioner-facing. Everything an engineer sees.

A piece is in one mode or the other. The failure case is a deck that opens cinematic and turns into a spec sheet on slide four with no change in treatment — decide which audience the document is for, then commit for its whole length.

03The symbol

The Aperture

Two opposing arrays of spectral blades, separated by a gate. Read one way it is a lens closing on a subject; read the other it is a spectrum plotted against wavelength. Both readings are true, which is the point.

The Aperture — construction GRID OFF
Named parts
ArchThe parabola both arrays stand on. It rises 40u from centre to outer blade and is the reason the symbol reads as a lens rather than a bar chart.
CrownThe upper array. Seventeen blades, tallest at the outer edges, collapsing to a 12u nub at centre.
BedThe lower array. Seventeen blades, inverted — deepest at centre, shortest at the edges.
GateThe clear band between crown and bed, riding the arch. Fixed at 32u. The gate never closes and never widens.
RampA single vertical gradient sampled across the full symbol height, so every blade takes its colour from where it sits, not from an index.

Construction

The symbol is generated, not drawn. Given the unit u — one two-hundredth of the symbol's bounding height — every dimension follows from the nine values below and two curves. Any redraw must reproduce these exactly.

ParameterValueGoverns
Blade count, per array17Odd, so a single blade sits on the centre axis and symmetry is verifiable
Pitch23uCentre-to-centre spacing, constant across both arrays
Blade width16u at baseLeaves a 5u channel after the round join; narrower and the symbol fills in at small sizes
Bounding box386 × 200uAspect 1.93 : 1. Never letterboxed or padded to another ratio.
Arch rise40uBoth arrays sit on axis(t) = 89 + 40t², t measured −1 to 1 across the width
Gate32u, constantCrown baseline to bed baseline, centred on the arch
Crown height12u → 111uSmoothstep: 12 + 99·S(|t|) where S(x) = x²(3−2x)
Bed height95u → 12uParabolic: 95 − 83t². Deepest at centre.
Tip radius1uApplied as a round join. Sharp points fringe on screen and clog in print.

The smoothstep is deliberate. A linear falloff produces a bar chart; a power curve produces a spike. x²(3−2x) — the same ease used in every shader ramp on our own stage — eases out of the centre and eases into the outer blade, which is what gives the crown its optical curve. It is the one place in this identity where the maths of the work shows up in the mark.

What changed from the first draft

01
True symmetryBlade heights in the draft differed by up to 9u between mirrored pairs, and the arrays had no shared centre. Both arrays are now 17 blades on one axis, mirrored exactly.
02
Regularised pitchSpacing drifted from 16 to 18 units across the width. Pitch is now constant at 23u, so the symbol holds its rhythm when scaled down.
03
A constant gateThe clear band narrowed from 33u at the edges to 21u at centre, which read as a drawing error rather than a device. It is now fixed at 32u and rides the arch.
04
One ramp, not manyColour was assigned per blade and repeated unpredictably down the bed. A single vertical gradient now spans the symbol, so the sequence is fixed and reproducible in any tool.
05
A light-ground rampThe original ramp opens on pale yellow, which is invisible on white. Spectrum Deep replaces it below — see section 06.
06
A lift for the logotypeGamma Violet at #9721B0 sits at 3.0:1 on Void. A lifted violet is now specified for dark grounds at small sizes.

The typography of the logotype is unchanged — it was already right. Everything above is geometry, colour and reproduction.

On the glyph. Gamma is also the transfer function that maps light to code value — the single most consequential curve in our work. Borrowing the letter as the small-size mark is not decoration; it is the one piece of the identity that means something specific to a colourist.

Choosing between A and B. Horizontal is the default and should be used wherever the container is wider than 2.6:1. Below that, switch to stacked rather than shrinking the horizontal lockup to fit — a lockup that clears the minimum size but sits in a sliver of white space reads as an afterthought.

06Grounds

The logo on white, and everywhere else

The symbol's ramp opens on a pale yellow that scores 1.2:1 against white — effectively invisible. So the identity carries two ramps, not one. Switch by ground, never by preference.

Void #080808 — primary ground RAMP: SPECTRUM PRIME

The two ramps

Spectrum Prime — dark grounds only

Photon #F5EC9A → Signal #48A0F2 → Index #4A5FE0 → Pulse #E63DA4 → Gamma Violet #9721B0. Stops at 0 / 28 / 46 / 72 / 100 %, sampled vertically across the symbol's bounding box.

Spectrum Deep — light grounds only

Ochre #7A6208 → Signal Deep #1265C4 → Index Deep #3A4FD0 → Pulse Deep #B0106F → Gamma Violet #9721B0. Same stop positions, same hue sequence, every stop at 5:1 or better on white.

Ground rules

G1
Dark ground → Spectrum Prime, logotype in Gamma Violet Lift #B84BD6The core violet is a light-ground colour. On Void it reaches only 3.0:1; the lift takes it to 4.8:1 and holds the hue. XRS keeps the Mica tail, rose to silver.
G2
Light ground → Spectrum Deep, logotype in Gamma Violet #9721B0 and Ink #171320The Mica tail fades into white and must not be used. On light grounds XRS is solid Ink — the two-tone effect is a dark-ground device.
G3
Brand violet ground → full reverse, whiteNo gradient at all. The symbol and logotype both go to solid white. Nothing else sits on a violet field.
G4
Photography → full reverse, white, with a scrimPlace on the quietest quarter of the frame and apply a 0–45% black linear scrim behind the clear-space box if local contrast drops below 4.5:1.
G5
One colour → single ink, no rampEngraving, embroidery, laser, fax, newsprint, single-colour merch. #F3F0F7 on dark, #171320 on light. Never a mid grey.
G6
Never place the gradient symbol on a mid-toneGrounds between L* 30 and L* 70 kill both ramps. Go dark, go light, or go single ink.
07Misuse

Eight ways to break it

Every one of these has happened to an identity like this one, usually inside a deck template, usually on a Friday.

01 ×Do not stretch, condense or scale non-uniformly. Ever.
02 ×Do not rotate. The Aperture has a horizon.
03 ×Do not recolour to a client's or partner's palette.
04 ×No shadows, glows, bevels or outer strokes.
05 ×Do not place the gradient lockup on a mid-tone or busy ground.
06 ×No outlined or hollow versions. Solid fill only.
07 ×Do not crowd. Clear space is 1×G minimum, always.
Gamma XRS
08 ×Do not reset the logotype in another typeface. It is drawn artwork.

Also prohibited without written approval from Brand & Communications: locking the Aperture up with a third-party mark, animating the logotype letterforms, using the symbol as a repeating pattern at small scale, and cropping the Aperture to a partial array.

08Colour

A palette that is also a spec

The core is one violet. The spectrum is five ordered stops that exist to be sampled, not scattered. The neutrals are biased toward violet so nothing on a Gamma XRS page reads as generic grey. Click any swatch to copy its hex.

Core
The Spectrum — five ordered stops

Order is fixed. When the spectrum encodes data series, assign them 01 → 05 in sequence and stop at five; a sixth series means the chart is wrong, not that the palette is short.

Colour outside sRGB

Display P3

The spectrum was authored in sRGB and clips safely into P3. On wide-gamut LED walls and grading displays, convert rather than re-pick — Pulse and Photon will otherwise drift toward the panel's primaries.

Print — CMYK

Gamma Violet does not reproduce in four-colour process. On any piece where the violet carries the brand, specify Pantone 2603 C as a fifth colour. If the budget is four-colour only, use the single-ink lockup in Ink.

On the wall

Never display the brand ramp as a full-screen background on an LED volume during a shoot. It contaminates practical spill and will show up in the plate. Brand content on the wall is for stills and stand-downs only.

09Typography

Three faces, strictly divided

The logotype is drawn artwork and is never a typeface you can install. Everything else runs on Archivo and JetBrains Mono — one variable grotesque with a real width axis, and one monospace that carries every number we publish.

Setting rules

T1
Width is hierarchyExpanded (112–118) for display and headings; normal (100) for body; never expanded below 20px, where it turns to mush.
T2
Measure 60–70 charactersBody copy never runs the full width of a panel. Set a max-width, not a column count.
T3
Negative tracking scales with size−2.5% at display, −1% at heading, 0 at body. Positive tracking only on uppercase mono labels, at 16–18%.
T4
Sentence case everywhere except labelsHeadlines are sentence case. All-caps belongs to mono labels and the logotype, nowhere else.
T5
Tabular numerals in any columnfont-variant-numeric: tabular-nums on every table, spec sheet and readout.
T6
Two weights per surfacePick a display weight and a body weight and hold them. A slide with four weights on it has no hierarchy, only noise.

Fallback stack where Archivo is unavailable (email, Office templates, client systems): Archivo, "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif. Mono fallback: "JetBrains Mono", ui-monospace, "SF Mono", Menlo, Consolas, monospace.

11Art direction

Show the frame, credit the rig

Our imagery has a specific problem to solve: everyone in this industry photographs the same blue-lit LED wall from the same three-quarter angle. We do not.

DO
Lead with the finished frameThe first image in any piece is the shot, presented full-bleed and un-annotated. The stage photograph comes second, as evidence.
DO
Shoot the crew, not the kitHands on a colour panel, a DP at the eyepiece, a stage manager on comms. People at work reads as capability; empty equipment reads as a rental listing.
DO
Use the plate as a light sourceWhere the wall appears, let its content light the foreground. Colour spill on faces and floor is the whole argument for the technique.
DO
Pair image with numberEvery hero image carries a mono caption: stage, lens, frame rate, date. Specificity is the personality.
NO
The three-quarter wall shotWide, empty volume, blue gradient, one figure for scale. It is the stock photograph of this industry.
NO
Grid and wireframe overlaysCyan wireframes on a face say "the future" and nothing else. We show real interfaces or nothing.
NO
Heavy brand gradingDo not push imagery toward violet to make it look on-brand. We sell colour accuracy; a violet-tinted portfolio undermines the claim.
NO
Generated or composited platesEvery frame we publish was captured. If it needs a disclaimer, it does not go out.
Grade

Neutral, contrast-forward, black point set to L* 4–6 rather than crushed. Shadows stay slightly cool; skin is not warmed. Grain is acceptable; halation and bloom are not.

Crop & caption

Hero imagery at 2.39:1 or 2:1. Supporting imagery at 3:2. Captions sit below-left in mono at 11px, 16% tracking, Haze — format: STAGE 04 · VOLUME ONE · 32MM · 23.976.

13Voice

Say the number

Our writing has one tell: it commits. Where a competitor writes "industry-leading latency", we write 4.2 ms and name the measurement point.

Sentence shape

Answer first, reasoning second. Average sentence under 18 words. One idea per sentence; if it needs a semicolon it needs a full stop.

Words we don't use

seamless · revolutionary · unlock · empower · game-changing · immersive · next-generation · leverage · solutions · magic

Words that are ours

plate · gate · final pixel · in tolerance · on the day · the floor · deterministic · measured at — borrowed from the set, not from marketing.

Voice by mode

Mode A — Studio

Second person, present tense, short. Talks about the shot and the schedule. Credits the director and the DP by name. Never explains how the pipeline works unless asked.

Mode B — Technology

First person plural, declarative, precise. Names versions, tolerances and failure modes. States what does not work as readily as what does. Assumes the reader has shipped something.
