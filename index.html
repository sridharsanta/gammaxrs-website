Why Gamma XRS

The stage and the software are one team.

Camera tracking, colour management, scene delivery — we wrote them, we run them, and we fix them standing on the floor. On your production we are the only party who owns both the volume and the code driving it, and that changes what happens on the day something goes wrong.

01 One team, no hand-off When the tracking drifts at eleven at night, the person who wrote it is standing on the floor. Not a ticket in another time zone.
02 Colour is a contract Our pipeline is specified in hex, Rec.709 and P3, and it is deterministic from scene file to sensor. The grade is agreed before the camera rolls and it survives to delivery.
03 Photoreal is a measurement We publish our colour maths, our latency and our tracking tolerance. Every figure we claim is one somebody on the floor can reproduce.
04 We say what will not work Before you book, not after. If a scene needs hard sun or deep water we tell you, and we tell you what we would do instead.

Before you book

Where the wall is the wrong answer.

Virtual production is a technique, not a religion. Four things we say out loud at the recce, because finding them out on the day costs you a day.

Send us the boards and the lens list. We will tell you which setups belong on the wall, which belong on a location, and which we would split.

Send us a scene
01
Hard sun An LED wall is bright; it is not daylight. For a hard sun key we light it practically and give the wall everything behind it.
02
The wall has an edge Sixty feet is wide, not infinite. A frame that pans past the ends is a location day, and we will tell you so.
03
Water and heavy refraction Rain bars and large water surfaces read the wall straight back at you. Some of that we solve on the floor; some of it belongs in a tank.
04
Your glass, tested first Moiré is a property of a lens against a panel, not a rumour. Bring the lens set to the recce and we will put it on the wall before the shoot day.

How a shoot runs

Five stages, and you see the frame at every one.

Nothing here is a surprise on the day. The decisions that cost money — which setups belong on the wall, what the environment looks like, how the wall sits against your key — are all made before the unit arrives.

01

Recce and camera test

You come to the floor with your DP and your lens set. We put your reference on the wall and shoot a test on your glass. You decide on footage rather than on a deck.

Before you book
02

Environment build

Our real-time artists build or adapt the world with your production designer. You sign it off running on the wall, at size — not as a still in an email.

Four to six weeks out
03

Tech and pre-light

A day on the floor with your gaffer. We balance the wall against your practical lighting, set the colour pipeline and lock the look you will shoot to.

The day before
04

Shoot day

Our crew runs the wall, the tracking and the desk alongside your unit. Environments change between setups in minutes. The director signs off the frame on the monitor.

On the day
05

Wrap and handover

Camera original, tracking data and the environment as delivered. Anything that does go to post starts from the same place you shot.

Environments

What goes on the wall.

Three routes, and the right one depends on how much the frame has to move rather than how much it has to look like.

Built for the script

A real-time environment our artists build with your production designer. Full parallax, full camera movement, lit to match your key.

Adapted from your reference

You arrive with a location scout, a matte or a stills library. We rebuild it as a plate that holds up to the moves you actually need.

Plate playback

Captured footage — driving plates, skies, crowds — played back in sync. The cheapest route when the camera is locked or nearly so.

Honest limits

What this technique will not do.

All four of these come up at the recce, not on the shoot day. If a setup belongs on a location we will say so while it is still cheap to move it.

01
Hard sunlight1000 nits is bright for a wall and dim for the sun. Sun keys come from a practical fixture; the wall does everything behind.
02
Frames that leave the wallSixty feet is wide, not infinite. A pan that finds the end of the volume is a location day.
03
Water and heavy atmosRain bars and standing water in front of an LED wall is a conversation about panel protection first and a shot second.
04
Very long lenses on a wide platePast a point the wall's pitch starts to show. We will tell you which lenses are safe on the test day.
Questions we get

Before you book.

How far out should we book?

Environment build is the long pole, not the stage. A bespoke environment wants four to six weeks from approved reference. A plate playback day can turn around in a week. [Current lead time — to confirm]

Can we shoot a test before committing?

Yes, and we would rather you did. Bring your camera and two lenses, we put your reference on the wall, and you leave with footage. It is the only honest way to decide.

Do you work with our VFX vendor?

Routinely. We hand over camera tracking data, the environment as delivered, and the on-set colour pipeline so anything that does go to post starts from the same place.

What does a day cost?

It depends on how much of the environment is built versus adapted, and how many days the wall is held. Send a shot list and we will come back with a range rather than a headline number. [Rate card — to confirm]

Can we shoot vertical?

Yes. For short-form vertical work the Atelier Stage is usually the better economics — same pipeline, a room sized to the format.

Questions we get

Before you book.

Can I bring my own camera?

Yes, and most people do. A phone works for social formats; a cinema camera gets more out of the wall. Either way the operator will set the wall to your shutter and frame rate.

How many environments in a day?

As many as your shot list has, if they are already built. Switching is a playback change, not a set change.

Can two people be on camera?

Comfortably. Past three the room starts to dictate your blocking, and that is the point where the Signature Stage is worth the difference.

Do you edit?

Not as standard. You leave with camera original and a proxy. [Post options — to confirm]

The premise

The audience is not the hard part any more. The production is.

Creators who have spent years learning what their audience watches hit the same wall moving up a format: the idea needs a crew, a stage and a pipeline, and those are priced for studios. So the work stays small, or it gets made badly once and abandoned.

We would rather spend stage downtime on those projects than on nothing. This is not sponsorship and it is not a competition — it is a working relationship with a small number of people each year.

What you get

Stage accessTime on the Atelier Stage, and on the Signature Stage where the project earns it, with the operating crew included.
02
The crew you cannot hire yetA producer, a technical director and real-time artists working on your project with you, not for a fee.
03
Format developmentHonest work on structure, length and pilot strategy. What travels from a feed to a commission is rarely the same cut.
04
Routes outIntroductions to broadcasters, platforms and distribution partners when the work is ready to be shown to them.
What we look for

Three things, in this order.

  • An audience you built yourself, at any size, that returns
  • An idea that is one size too big for you right now
  • A reason it needs a volume rather than a location
  • Somebody on your side who can run a shoot day
What we do not take

So you do not waste an application.

  • Work that would be cheaper and better shot on a location
  • Brand campaigns with a client already attached — that is a booking
  • Projects where the stage is the idea rather than the setting
  • Anything that needs us to take the channel or the IP
How it works

From application to a finished pilot.

  1. Apply

    Send the channel, the idea, and what is stopping you making it. Three paragraphs is plenty. We read everything and we reply either way.

  2. Development session

    A working session on the floor with a producer and a technical director, to find out whether the idea and the technique actually suit each other. Some do not, and we will say so on the day.

  3. Shoot the pilot

    We allocate stage time and crew. You direct. The work stays yours, and so does the channel it goes out on.

  4. Take it to market

    Where it fits, we introduce it to partners who commission this kind of work — as co-producers, not as your vendor.
